echo-tunes

YouTube Music on your Echo, from your own server.

An open-source Alexa skill and web player you host yourself. Ask your Echo for a song and it plays, then keeps going with a radio of similar music. Or search, browse and play from your browser, or the phone app. One Docker container, no AWS account, no Amazon password.

What you get

Ask by voice

Songs, artists, “play radio for …”, next, pause, shuffle, “what's playing”.

Endless radio

Every song starts a YouTube Music radio; the queue tops itself up.

Your library

Optional Google sign-in: liked songs, your playlists and personal mixes.

Web player

Search songs, albums, artists and playlists, browse charts and moods. Play in the browser or queue on an Echo.

Phone app

Install it from your browser: its own icon, full screen, lock-screen controls.

Private dashboard

Now playing on every Echo, 15-day history, stats, a setup checklist and a test-play button.

One container

Docker image for PCs, servers and Raspberry Pi (amd64 and arm64). Flat memory use.

Secure by default

Every Alexa request is signature-checked; audio links are signed and expire.

How it works

  1. Run the echo-tunes container on an always-on computer and give it a public HTTPS address (Caddy on a cloud server, Cloudflare Tunnel, Tailscale Funnel or your own reverse proxy).
  2. Create a free, private skill in the Alexa developer console and point it at that address.
  3. Say “Alexa, ask my tunes to play …”. The Echo streams the song from your server.

Questions

Do I need YouTube Premium?

No. Signing in is optional and only adds your own library.

Is it in the Alexa Skill Store?

No. Each person runs a private copy on their own Amazon developer account, which is free.

Can it run on a cloud server?

Yes, but YouTube is stricter with data-centre addresses; a home server is the most reliable.

Can I control the Echo from the web player?

You can line songs up on any Echo. Alexa only lets a skill change playback when the Echo checks in, so your pick plays after the current song; pausing stays with your voice.
