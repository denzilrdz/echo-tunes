YouTube Music on your Echo, from your own server.

Ask your Echo for a song and it plays, then keeps going with a radio of similar music. Or pick songs on your phone and play them right there or on any Echo in the house. One Docker container, no AWS account, no Amazon password.

Everything you'd want from a music app. On your hardware.

Ask by voice

Songs, artists, “play radio for …”, next, pause, shuffle, “what's playing”. Works on every Echo.

Web player & phone app

Search, browse charts and moods, open albums and playlists. Install it from the browser: its own icon, lock-screen controls.

Play on any Echo

Pick a favourite (the living-room Echo, or this phone) and it's ready every time. Songs you pick play after the current one.

Endless radio

Every song starts a YouTube Music radio, and the queue tops itself up. It never just stops.

Your library

Optional Google sign-in on the server: liked songs, your playlists and personal mixes.

Private & self-hosted

History and stats stay on your server. Docker for PCs, servers and Raspberry Pi (amd64 and arm64).

Secure by default

Every Alexa request is signature-checked; audio links are signed and expire; the dashboard has a strict content policy.

Made to be monitored

Health checks, Prometheus metrics and copy-paste setups for Uptime Kuma and Home Assistant.

How it works

  1. Run the containerOn an always-on computer, and give it a public HTTPS address: Caddy, Cloudflare Tunnel, Tailscale Funnel or your own reverse proxy.
  2. Create a private skillA free skill in the Alexa developer console, pointed at that address. No AWS account, no Amazon password on the server.
  3. PlaySay “Alexa, ask my tunes to play …”, or open the web player on your phone and pick a song.

Questions

Do I need YouTube Premium?

No. Signing in is optional and only adds your own library.

Is it in the Alexa Skill Store?

No. Each person runs a private copy on their own Amazon developer account, which is free.

Can I control the Echo from my phone?

You can line songs up on any Echo. Alexa only lets a skill change playback when the Echo checks in, so your pick plays after the current song; pausing stays with your voice.

Can it run on a cloud server?

Yes, but YouTube is stricter with data-centre addresses; a home server is the most reliable.
